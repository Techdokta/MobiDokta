Read This First

Five questions to ask your insurer before you pay anyone.

Most rejected assessments are not rejected because the technician was wrong. They are rejected because nobody checked what the policy actually required. Ask these five questions first — it takes one phone call, and it can save you the whole fee.

Ask your insurer / broker

Send us the answers and we will scope the job exactly — or tell you we are not the right fit.

  1. Who is the insurer or underwriter, and is there a claim or reference number yet? The report must be addressed correctly and quote the claim reference, or it gets returned unprocessed.
  2. Have they specified what the report must contain, or issued a form? Some insurers have their own assessment template. If yours does, we complete theirs — not ours.
  3. Do they require a manufacturer-authorised assessor, or do they accept an independent qualified technician's report? This is the one that matters most. See the disclosure below.
  4. Is this a repair claim or a replacement / write-off claim? A repair claim needs an itemised parts-and-labour estimate. A write-off claim needs a pre-damage market value and an economic-repair conclusion. Different reports.
  5. Is there a submission deadline? If it is tight, take the fast-track tier — a late report can invalidate an otherwise valid claim.

Don't want to phone them? Forward us the insurer's email or claim letter on WhatsApp and we will read the requirements for you.

The Deliverable

What is actually in the report.

A signed PDF, emailed to you and to your insurer or broker if you ask us to. Same structure every time, so claims handlers know where to look — which is a large part of why reports get accepted first time.

1

Claim & owner identification

Insurer, underwriter, claim reference, policyholder details, date of loss, date of assessment, and the assessment reference number.

2

Device identity & IMEI verification

Make, model, storage, colour, IMEI / serial read directly off the device and cross-checked against the label and system. Confirms the assessed device is the insured device.

3

Blacklist & ownership status

IMEI blacklist check against the GSMA device registry, plus activation-lock and carrier-lock status. Flags a device reported stolen before a claim is paid on it.

4

Full hardware diagnostic

Display, digitiser, battery health and cycle count, cameras, speakers, microphones, sensors, Face ID / Touch ID, charging circuit, board-level inspection under magnification.

5

Software & firmware read-up

OS version, firmware state, diagnostic logs, panic and crash history, and evidence of prior repair or non-genuine parts — which insurers specifically look for.

6

Functional test matrix

Every subsystem tested and recorded as pass / fail / degraded, not described in prose. A claims handler can read the table in ten seconds.

7

Dated photographic evidence

Exterior from all faces, damage close-ups, IMEI / serial plate, internal board condition, corrosion or impact witness marks. Captioned and dated.

8

Damage cause-consistency opinion

Whether the physical evidence is consistent, partially consistent, or inconsistent with the reported cause of loss — stated as a technical opinion with the reasoning shown.

9

Itemised repair estimate

Line-by-line parts and labour, VAT shown separately, parts grade stated, with lead time. Priced so it can be approved as-is without a follow-up query.

10

Repairable or write-off conclusion

Economic-repair test against current pre-damage market value, with a clear recommendation: repair, replace, or write off — and the numbers behind it.

11

Data & POPIA handling record

What data was accessed, what was not, and confirmation that nothing was retained. Required for corporate and institutional claims.

12

Signed assessor declaration

Named technician, qualification, date, signature, and a declaration of independence — that we have no interest in the claim outcome.

What we will and won't put in a report

We report what we find. If the damage is consistent with what you told your insurer, the report says so plainly and in technical detail — and that is worth far more to your claim than any favour we could do you. If the evidence points somewhere else, the report says that too.

We say this on the page rather than in the small print because it is the entire basis of the service. A signed assessment is a document an insurer releases money against. The moment an assessor is known to write reports to order, every claim that assessor has ever touched gets re-opened — which helps nobody, least of all the honest policyholder. Our independence is what makes your report useful.

We also have no financial interest in the claim outcome. Whether your device is repaired or written off, our fee is the same.

Questions

Insurance assessment FAQ.

The things people ask before they book — answered properly.

Are you an Apple Authorised Service Provider?
No, and we won't imply it on a document an insurer relies on. MobiDokta is an independent repair and assessment provider; reports are signed as an independent qualified technician's assessment, backed by iFixit repair standards, REWA micro-soldering standards and Mechatronics engineering qualification. Most South African insurers accept an independent report and many prefer the turnaround — but if your policy specifically requires a manufacturer-authorised assessor, tell us and we will point you to the right channel instead of taking the job.
Why is an assessment R650 when a normal diagnosis is free?
Because they are different pieces of work. A free diagnosis tells you what is wrong so you can decide about a repair. An insurance assessment is 45–90 minutes of documented bench diagnostic, plus photographic evidence capture, market-value research, a written cause-consistency opinion, and a signed report that an insurer releases money against. The report carries our name and our liability — that is what the fee covers. If you then repair with us, the fee is credited against the repair.
How fast can I get the report?
Within 24 hours of us receiving the device on the fast-track tier (R850), or 2–3 working days on the standard tier (R650). If your insurer has set a deadline, tell us the date up front and take fast-track — a late report can invalidate a claim that was otherwise perfectly good.
My insurer sent me their own assessment form. Can you fill that in?
Yes — and that is usually the better route. Send us the form and we complete theirs rather than issuing ours, at the same price. If the form asks for something we cannot verify, we say so in writing on the form rather than guessing, and we tell you before you submit it.
I'm not in Pretoria. Can you still assess my device?
Yes, anywhere in South Africa. R199 flat for insured door-to-door collection — we book the courier, send you tracking on WhatsApp, assess the device and courier it back. You never deal with a courier company yourself. Build 1–2 days each way into your insurer's deadline.
Will the report say what I need it to say?
It will say what we find. If the damage is consistent with the cause you reported, the report states that plainly and in technical detail, which is far more useful to your claim than anything we could invent. If the evidence points elsewhere, it states that too. We sign these reports — an assessor known to write to order is worthless to insurers and therefore worthless to you.
What if my insurer rejects the report?
If it is rejected on format or because a required section was missing, we re-issue it corrected at no charge, and we speak to the claims handler directly to establish what they need. If it is rejected because they require an authorised assessor we told you about up front, we refund the assessment fee — that check should have happened before you paid.
Does the assessment erase my device or access my data?
No. An assessment is non-destructive — we read the system, run diagnostics and inspect the hardware. We do not restore, wipe or browse your content. What was accessed and what was not is recorded in the report's data-handling section. If the device needs to be powered up and you would rather remove your SIM or sign out first, do that before you send it.
The device is a total loss. What happens to it?
That is your call, or your insurer's. We can return it as-is, perform certified data erasure with a signed destruction certificate (R250), route it through licensed e-waste disposal with a disposal certificate (R150), or make you a salvage offer if the components still hold value. Written-off devices are often worth more than people assume.
Can you assess a device that was already repaired somewhere else?
Yes, and prior repair is one of the things the report specifically documents — non-genuine parts, previous board work and repair witness marks all show up in the diagnostic. Insurers look for this, so it is better that an independent report records it accurately than that they discover it later.
Do you assess devices for warranty disputes, not just insurance?
Yes. The same assessment works when a supplier has rejected a warranty claim and blamed the user, when a landlord or employer disputes responsibility for damage, or when a second-hand seller misrepresented a device. An independent technical finding is usually what breaks the deadlock.
We're a company with 40 damaged devices. How does that work?
Fleet rate from R450 per device on 5 or more, quoted on volume. You get an individual report per device plus a consolidated summary spreadsheet reconciled to your asset tags and serials. On-site assessment is available in Gauteng so the devices never leave your premises. Certified erasure and disposal certificates for the write-offs can be bundled in.
